Email delivery control plane for a CRM platform

A delivery control plane for a multi-tenant CRM: provider tenancy per customer, domain readiness checks, an event pipeline with replay, reputation monitoring with automatic throttles, and inbox-placement tests that can gate a campaign.

In one paragraph

A multi-tenant CRM platform sent email for many customers through copied provider credentials, shallow domain checks and synchronous event handling, and learned about reputation problems after the damage. I designed and built an additive email delivery control plane on Amazon SES: provider tenancy and pools per customer stream behind one send gateway, domain readiness checks across DKIM, custom MAIL FROM, SPF, DMARC and event wiring, a durable event pipeline with retries and replay, reputation monitoring with automatic throttle and hold for marketing traffic, inbox-placement seed tests that can gate a campaign, and an operations console.

The challenge

A CRM platform sent email for many customers through copied provider credentials, shallow domain checks and synchronous event handling. Reputation problems were found after the damage, and there was no way to isolate one customer’s traffic from another’s.

Approach

  • Separated who owns what: the platform operates provider accounts, pools and warm-up; a customer proves domain ownership, chooses protections and limits, and reads readiness and reputation signals.
  • Made the rollout additive: no existing table, route, send call or credential removed, with explicit credentials winning over central bindings during migration.
  • Protected marketing traffic without touching transactional: automatic throttle and hold apply to marketing streams only, and no automation clears a human hold.

What was built

  • Provider tenancy per customer stream with configuration sets, identity association and shared, isolated or transactional pools, behind one idempotent send gateway.
  • Domain readiness evaluated per stream across DKIM, custom MAIL FROM, SPF, DMARC, identity association and event wiring, with advisory gaps and blocking failures told apart.
  • Delivery events persisted before acknowledgement, processed by a worker with retries, a dead-letter queue, lag reporting and audited replay.
  • Reputation monitoring from seven-day leading indicators and Google Postmaster signals, with customer thresholds, an alert lifecycle, warm-up capacity, automatic throttle and hold.
  • Inbox-placement seed tests returning placement by mailbox provider, optionally gating a campaign, and tiered email validation with free local checks first.
  • An operations view of provider bindings, queue health, customer health, incidents, pools and audited controls.

Outcome

Customers see readiness, reputation and placement before they send, operations can isolate, throttle or pause a stream with an audit trail, and the migration path keeps existing sending intact until each piece is switched on.

Background

When one platform sends on behalf of many customers, deliverability is shared. A single customer’s bad list can damage an IP pool everyone uses, and a provider credential copied into each tenant means nobody can see the whole picture. A control plane is the layer that owns sending centrally while letting each customer see and control their own domain.

The problem in detail

Credentials were copied from the platform into tenants. Domain verification checked less than mailbox providers actually require. Delivery events were handled synchronously, so a slow webhook could lose them. Reputation was handled reactively, after complaints had already landed. There was no way to give one customer dedicated capacity or to pause one stream without touching the rest.

How the work was done

Ownership was drawn first. The platform operates provider accounts, tenants, configuration sets, pools, warm-up and incidents. A customer proves domain ownership, chooses protections and limits, reviews readiness and reputation signals, and can request dedicated delivery. The customer never holds provider credentials.

The rollout is additive by rule: no existing table, route, send call or credential is removed; an explicit credential keeps winning over a central binding during migration; existing campaigns are not gated until a customer turns gating on; and a missing readiness snapshot never blocks traffic, only a current blocked state does. Automatic controls apply to marketing streams only, transactional mail is protected from a marketing hold, and no automated process clears a human pause.

What was delivered

Provider tenancy per customer stream with configuration sets, identity association and shared, isolated or transactional pools, wrapped by one idempotent send gateway that personal mailboxes also write through. Domain readiness evaluated per domain and stream across six checks, with advisory gaps and blocking failures told apart. Delivery events persisted before acknowledgement, then processed by a worker with backoff, a dead-letter queue after repeated failure, lag reporting and audited replay. Reputation monitoring from seven-day leading indicators and Google Postmaster signals with customer thresholds, an alert lifecycle, warm-up capacity, automatic throttle and automatic hold. Inbox-placement seed tests returning placement by provider, optionally gating a campaign, and tiered validation that uses free local checks first. An operations console for provider bindings, queue health, customer health, incidents, pools and audited controls, deployed with infrastructure as code and a staged release tracker.

Results

Customers see readiness, reputation and placement before they send. Operations can isolate, throttle or pause a stream with an audit trail. The migration path keeps existing sending intact until each piece is switched on.

What this means for your team

  • Decide who owns sending before you build. A control plane is an ownership model with code attached.
  • Persist delivery events before you acknowledge them. Lost bounces become future complaints.
  • Automate throttles for marketing, never for transactional, and never let automation clear a human hold.

Questions about this project

What is an email delivery control plane?

The platform layer that owns provider accounts, tenancy, pools, warm-up, events and reputation centrally, while exposing readiness, limits and placement signals to each customer for their own domains.

What are the domain readiness checks?

DKIM, a custom MAIL FROM with its MX record, SPF, DMARC, the association of the identity with the tenant, and the wiring of delivery events. Advisory gaps produce an action-required state; a mandatory failure blocks the route.

How does inbox-placement testing work?

A seed test sends the campaign to monitored mailboxes at the major providers and reports where it landed. A customer can choose to make a passing result a condition for sending.
