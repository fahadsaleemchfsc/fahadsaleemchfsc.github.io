HubSpotMake

Customer Success activity tracking

The date of the last real touch from the Customer Success team stamped on every contact, automatically.

Work
HubSpot integration with Make
Platform
HubSpot, Make
Stack
HubSpot, Make, HubSpot API, Workflows
Client
Private, by agreement

In one paragraph

Managers wanted to see when any member of the Customer Success team last contacted each customer. HubSpot workflows cannot re-trigger on every logged email, so I split the job: a HubSpot workflow stamps the date for calls, a Make scenario watches email engagements and stamps it for emails, and a one-off batched backfill filled in history. Enrolment is by team membership, so new starters are covered without edits.

The challenge

Managers wanted to see when a customer was last contacted by anyone on the team. HubSpot workflows cannot trigger on a logged email more than once per contact, and the data needed to reach back into history.

Approach

  • Split the problem: a HubSpot workflow for calls, a Make scenario for emails, and a one-off backfill for history.
  • Enrolled by team membership rather than named users so new starters are covered without edits.
  • Designed the backfill in batches so it reads companies and owners once instead of once per contact.

What was built

  • A Make scenario watching email engagements, with the sales-email scope approved through HubSpot’s app governance.
  • A HubSpot workflow writing the activity date for calls.
  • A written API brief for the backfill, with smoke tests before anything touched live records.
  • A batched owner backfill that maps free-text names to HubSpot users on contacts and their companies.

Outcome

Every contact carries a reliable last-activity date, reporting on neglected accounts works, and the automation keeps itself current as the team changes.

Background

A last-activity date sounds like something a CRM should already have. HubSpot does record last activity, but not in a way that distinguishes a touch from a specific team, and a workflow that triggers on a logged email fires once per contact, not every time. The team wanted a reliable, team-specific date they could report on.

The problem in detail

Three sources of activity had to be covered: calls, which HubSpot can trigger on; emails, which it cannot re-trigger on; and the historical record, which needed a one-off pass. The data also had to reach companies, not only contacts, so that account-level reports on neglected customers would work.

How the work was done

Each source got the right tool. Calls are handled by a HubSpot workflow. Emails are handled by a Make scenario that watches email engagements through the API and writes the date when the sender is on the Customer Success team. The sales-email scope the scenario needed was approved through HubSpot's app governance rather than worked around.

The backfill was designed in batches so that companies and owners are read once rather than once per contact, and free-text owner names from the historical data were mapped to HubSpot users. A written API brief and smoke tests on test records came before anything touched live data.

What was delivered

A Make scenario watching email engagements with the approved scope. A HubSpot workflow writing the activity date for calls. A written API brief for the backfill with the smoke tests. A batched owner backfill that maps names to HubSpot users on contacts and their companies.

Results

Every contact carries a reliable last-activity date from the Customer Success team. Reports on neglected accounts work. Because enrolment is by team membership, the automation keeps itself current as people join and leave.

What this means for your team

  • Match each event source to the tool that can see it. One tool rarely covers calls, emails and history.
  • Enrol by team membership, never by named users, so the automation survives staff changes.
  • Design backfills in batches that read shared records once. It is the difference between minutes and days.

Questions about this project

Why not do everything in a HubSpot workflow?

Because a workflow enrols a contact once per trigger condition. It cannot re-run for each new logged email, so emails needed an integration that watches engagements as they happen.

Why Make rather than custom code for the email part?

Make can watch HubSpot engagements and write properties with a few modules, and the client could see and edit the scenario. Code was reserved for the backfill, where batching mattered.

Is the sales-email scope safe to grant?

It was requested through HubSpot's app governance, scoped to the one scenario, and reviewed by the portal admin before approval.
