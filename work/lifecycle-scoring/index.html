HubSpotMarketing Hub

Lifecycle and lead-scoring redesign

A portal full of overlapping routing workflows rebuilt into one lifecycle and scoring model.

Work
HubSpot Marketing Hub redesign
Platform
HubSpot, Marketing Hub
Stack
HubSpot Marketing Hub, Lead scoring, Workflows, Dashboards
Client
Private, by agreement

In one paragraph

A HubSpot portal had accumulated dozens of workflows that stamped lifecycle stages and routed leads, several of them in conflict. I inventoried every workflow, documented the ones that touched lifecycle, scoring, routing or deal creation, flagged each conflict against a target design, and delivered one lifecycle and scoring model: fit and engagement scored separately, routing driven by score bands, and a plan for existing contacts so history is not rewritten.

The challenge

Years of additions had left dozens of workflows stamping lifecycle stages and routing leads, several of them in conflict. Nobody could say with confidence why a lead landed where it did.

Approach

  • Inventoried every workflow in the portal and documented the ones that touch lifecycle, scoring, routing or deal creation.
  • Flagged each conflict against the target design before proposing a single change.
  • Designed scoring on fit and engagement separately, with routing driven by score bands rather than hand-maintained lists.

What was built

  • A workflow audit with the logic, owner and recommendation for each workflow that mattered.
  • A blueprint covering lifecycle stages, the scoring model, routing rules and the target set of workflows.
  • A plan for handling existing contacts so historical stages are not silently rewritten.
  • A lead-scoring dashboard for the sales team.

Outcome

The client has one documented model to build against, a short list of decisions to make, and a dashboard that shows where leads sit and why.

Background

Lifecycle stages in HubSpot describe where a contact is in the journey, from subscriber to customer. Lead scoring adds a number that says how good a lead looks. Routing decides who gets it. In a portal that has grown for years, these three are usually implemented by many small workflows written by different people at different times.

The problem in detail

Several workflows were setting lifecycle stages on overlapping conditions, so the stage a contact ended up in depended on which workflow ran last. Routing used hand-maintained lists that drifted from the org chart. When sales asked why a lead had landed with them, marketing could not answer with confidence, and that erodes trust in every lead that follows.

How the work was done

The first deliverable was an inventory: every workflow in the portal, with the ones that touched lifecycle, scoring, routing or deal creation documented in detail, including their logic, their owner and a recommendation. Each conflict was flagged against the target design before a single change was proposed, so the client could see the cost of the current state rather than take it on faith.

The new model scores fit and engagement separately. Fit is about who the contact is: role, company, industry. Engagement is about what they do: visits, opens, replies, form fills. Routing reads the combined score bands rather than named lists, so adding a rep or changing a territory is a configuration change, not a rewrite.

What was delivered

A workflow audit with the logic, owner and recommendation for each workflow that mattered. A blueprint covering the lifecycle stages, the scoring model, the routing rules and the target set of workflows that replaces the sprawl. A plan for existing contacts, so that historical stages are migrated deliberately rather than silently rewritten by the new rules. A lead-scoring dashboard for the sales team showing where leads sit and why.

Results

The client has one documented model to build against, a short list of decisions that only they can make, and a dashboard that explains each lead's position. The audit itself became the reference for the portal.

What this means for your team

  • Inventory before redesign. You cannot replace what you have not listed.
  • Score fit and engagement separately. A combined number hides why a lead is hot.
  • Route on score bands and team membership, never on hand-maintained lists.

Questions about this project

What is the difference between lifecycle stage and lead status in HubSpot?

Lifecycle stage is the broad journey position, from subscriber through lead, marketing qualified lead, sales qualified lead, opportunity and customer. Lead status is the sales team's working state within the lead stages, such as new, attempting contact or connected.

Why not just delete the conflicting workflows?

Because each one was there for a reason somebody once had. The audit records the reason, the owner and the recommendation, so deletions are decisions rather than accidents.

How are existing contacts handled when the model changes?

With a written migration plan: which historical stages are kept, which are recalculated, and in what order, so reporting history stays meaningful.
