Sending infrastructure for a customer reactivation campaign

Authenticated sending on Amazon SES for a WordPress store, with bounces and complaints wired back into the CRM, a cleaned list, and a first reactivation campaign sent in controlled segments.

Work
Email deliverability and sending infrastructure
Platform
Deliverability, Amazon SES
Stack
Amazon SES, FluentCRM and FluentSMTP, WordPress and WooCommerce, SNS webhooks, Google Postmaster Tools

Private, by agreement

In one paragraph

An online store wanted to email its existing customers about a relaunch from a domain with little marketing history, through basic WordPress mail with no bounce or complaint handling. I audited the domain and its reputation first, then moved sending onto Amazon SES inside the client’s own account with SPF, DKIM, DMARC and a custom return path verified, wired bounce and complaint events back into FluentCRM so affected contacts drop out automatically, added one-click unsubscribe headers, cleaned and segmented the list, connected Google Postmaster Tools, seed-tested across the major mailbox providers, and planned the first campaign as a staged rollout with pause criteria.

The challenge

An online store wanted to email its existing customers about a relaunch, from a sending domain with little marketing history, through basic WordPress mail that had no bounce or complaint handling. Sending the whole list at once would have risked the domain before the relaunch began.

Approach

  • Audited first: DNS and authentication, domain and sender reputation, blocklist signals and prior sending history, with a written recommendation before any change.
  • Kept sending inside the client’s own cloud account through a dedicated user scoped to email only, and left DNS changes to the client’s team on written instruction.
  • Treated the rollout as reputation-sensitive: the most recent and engaged customers first, expanding in segments with clear criteria for pausing.

What was built

  • FluentCRM on FluentSMTP with Amazon SES: SPF, DKIM, DMARC, alignment, a custom return path and TLS verified, with the store’s transactional mail confirmed unaffected.
  • Bounce, complaint and delivery events from a configuration set to SNS and back into the CRM, so a hard bounce or a complaint drops the contact from future sends, with complaints permanent and account-level suppression kept on.
  • One-click List-Unsubscribe headers on every campaign, and marketing consent kept separate from transactional mail.
  • A list review for duplicates, invalid addresses, prior bounces, unsubscribes and complaints, with recency and engagement segmentation.
  • Google Postmaster Tools connected and seed tests across the major mailbox providers before launch, plus a handover record for the internal team.

Outcome

An authenticated, monitored sending setup in the client’s own account, a first campaign that goes out in controlled segments with pause criteria, and a handover the internal team can run future campaigns from.

Background

Deliverability is whether email reaches the inbox, and it is earned by a sending domain over time. A domain with little marketing history has no reputation to spend, so the first large campaign decides how mailbox providers treat everything that follows. Basic WordPress mail gives none of the controls that protect a domain: no authentication beyond what the host offers, no bounce handling, no complaint handling, no suppression.

The problem in detail

The store had a list of existing customers, a relaunch to announce and an offer to make. What it lacked was a sending setup that would survive the send. Without bounce and complaint handling, every invalid address and every annoyed recipient would be hit again on the next campaign. Without authentication and a one-click unsubscribe, the major mailbox providers would treat the mail as suspect from the first message.

How the work was done

The audit came before any change: DNS and authentication records, domain and sender reputation, blocklist signals and whatever sending history existed, ending in a written recommendation on whether Amazon SES was the right fit. Sending stays inside the client’s own cloud account through a dedicated user scoped to email only, and DNS changes are made by the client’s team on written instruction, so the client keeps control of its infrastructure.

The rollout is based on the domain’s actual condition rather than a generic warm-up schedule: the most recent and most engaged customers first, expanding in segments, with bounces, complaints, unsubscribes and authentication monitored and clear criteria for pausing before continuing.

What was delivered

FluentCRM configured on FluentSMTP with Amazon SES, with SPF, DKIM, DMARC, domain alignment, a custom return path and TLS verified, and the store’s transactional mail confirmed unaffected before and after. Bounce, complaint and delivery events published from a configuration set to SNS and received by the CRM, so a hard bounce or a complaint excludes the contact from future sends; complaints are permanent and account-level suppression stays on as a backstop. One-click List-Unsubscribe headers on every campaign, with marketing consent kept separate from transactional mail. A list review for duplicates, invalid addresses, prior bounces, unsubscribes and complaints, segmented by recency and engagement. Google Postmaster Tools connected, seed tests across the major providers, and a handover record covering everything configured.

Results

An authenticated, monitored sending setup in the client’s own account, a first campaign that goes out in controlled segments with pause criteria, and a handover the internal team can run future campaigns from.

What this means for your team

  • Audit the domain before you configure anything. Reputation is the constraint, and you cannot see it from inside the CRM.
  • Wire bounces and complaints back into the CRM automatically. A suppression list maintained by hand is a suppression list that is out of date.
  • Roll out by engagement, in segments, with pause criteria written down. The first campaign on a new domain is a reputation event, not a send.

Questions about this project

Why Amazon SES rather than the CRM’s built-in sending?

SES keeps sending in the client’s own account, gives full control of authentication and return path, and publishes bounce and complaint events that can be wired back into the CRM. The audit confirms the fit before implementation.

What is a one-click List-Unsubscribe header?

A header that lets mailbox providers show an unsubscribe button that works with a single click. Gmail and Yahoo require it from bulk senders, and honouring it quickly protects the domain from complaints.

How do you warm up a sending domain?

By sending to the most engaged recipients first in small segments, watching bounces, complaints and authentication results, and expanding only while the signals stay clean. The schedule follows the domain’s real condition, not a template.
