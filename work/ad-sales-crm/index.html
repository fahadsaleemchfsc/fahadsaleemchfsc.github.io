Ad-sales CRM for a media publisher

A deal pipeline built around insertion orders, with commissions and fulfilment handoff automated.

In one paragraph

A media publisher selling advertising space moved its sales operation from spreadsheets and email into HubSpot Sales Hub. I modelled the business first, then built a deal pipeline where one deal is one insertion order, products carry the revenue year, commissions are stamped automatically from the owner and title, and a closed-won deal hands itself to fulfilment. The result is a single pipeline sellers work from and a dashboard management reads by title and year.

The challenge

Advertising sales were tracked in spreadsheets and email. Renewals, commissions and fulfilment all depended on someone remembering, and there was no single view of what had been sold for which title and year.

Approach

  • Modelled the business before touching settings: company owner as the authority, one deal per insertion order, products and line items carrying the revenue year.
  • Kept the product catalogue broad so reporting stays stable from year to year.
  • Wrote the stage rules and quality checks down, agreed them, then built the automation to enforce them.

What was built

  • A deal pipeline with stage-entry rules and quality checks when an order is sent.
  • Commission stamping from the owner and title mapping, with an override for approved exceptions.
  • A closed-won handoff that flags the advertiser and creates the fulfilment task.
  • A renewal working list, seller views and a sales dashboard filtered by revenue year.
  • A historical import reconciled against the source, plus an as-built reference and seller guide.

Outcome

Sellers work from one pipeline with the next step always visible, and management sees sold, open and remaining-to-goal by title. A second phase is adding call queues, an approval stage and insertion-order tracking.

Background

Advertising sales has a shape most CRM templates do not expect. The unit of sale is an insertion order, which is a commitment to run advertising in a particular title for a particular period. The same advertiser buys again each year, often for several titles, and the people who sell, invoice and fulfil the order are different teams. Before this project, that whole chain lived in spreadsheets and inboxes.

The problem in detail

Renewals depended on a seller remembering that a contract was ending. Commission calculation depended on someone reconciling who sold what, for which title, at the end of a period. Fulfilment learned about a sale when an email arrived. Nobody could answer a simple question such as how much had been sold for one title in one year without building a spreadsheet to answer it.

How the work was done

I started with the model, not the settings. We agreed that the company owner in HubSpot is the authority for who gets credit, that every insertion order becomes exactly one deal, and that products and line items carry the revenue year so that reporting can be filtered by year without custom properties on every deal. The product catalogue was kept deliberately broad so that reporting stays stable when a title changes its packaging.

Stage rules and quality checks were written down in plain language and agreed with the sales lead before any automation existed. Only then were the workflows built to enforce them, so the automation reflects a process people had already accepted.

What was delivered

A deal pipeline with rules for entering each stage and quality checks that run when an order is sent. Commission stamping that reads the deal owner and the title mapping, with an override property for approved exceptions so the rule does not have to be bent by hand. A closed-won handoff that flags the advertiser as active and creates the fulfilment task with the details fulfilment needs. A renewal working list, per-seller views and a sales dashboard filtered by revenue year. The historical data was imported and reconciled against the source, and the build was documented as an as-built reference with a seller guide.

Results

Sellers work from one pipeline where the next step is always visible. Management sees sold, open and remaining-to-goal by title without asking anyone to compile it. A second phase is adding call queues, an approval stage and insertion-order tracking on the same model.

What this means for your team

  • Model the business before touching the CRM: decide what a deal is, who the authority is for credit, and where the reporting dimension lives.
  • Keep the product catalogue broad. Reporting that depends on a stable catalogue survives packaging changes.
  • Write stage rules as sentences and get them agreed before building workflows to enforce them.

Questions about this project

Why one deal per insertion order rather than one deal per advertiser?

Because the insertion order is what gets sold, invoiced and fulfilled. One deal per order means revenue, commission and fulfilment all attach to the thing they belong to, and the advertiser relationship lives on the company record.

How are commissions calculated in HubSpot?

A workflow stamps the commission on the deal from the owner and the title mapping. An override property exists for approved exceptions, and every override is visible in reporting.

Does this work on HubSpot Sales Hub Professional?

Yes. The build uses pipelines, products and line items, workflows and custom reports, all available on Sales Hub Professional.
