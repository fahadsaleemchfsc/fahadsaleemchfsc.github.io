Pre-send checks and delivery audit for a Marketing Cloud campaign

A pre-send checklist, seed tests and a production delivery audit for a Marketing Cloud campaign, so every send is reviewed before it goes and verified after it lands.

Work
Campaign QC and delivery audit
Platform
Marketing Cloud, Deliverability
Stack
Marketing Cloud Growth, Data Cloud, Salesforce Flow, SQL

Private, by agreement

In one paragraph

A healthcare marketing team was launching a new touch in Marketing Cloud to a reviewed audience while older flows stayed paused. I wrote a pre-send checklist and ran it before launch, covering audience membership, channel opt-in, do-not-contact and opt-out overlap, open opportunities, replied suppression and send history; sent personalised tests to a real inbox and inspected previews; confirmed the old flows were paused; verified every activation; and audited production delivery from raw engagement records, classifying every bounce and explaining every skipped profile.

The challenge

A healthcare marketing team was launching a new touch to a reviewed audience while older campaign flows stayed paused. One wrong send, a duplicate, or a message to an opted-out contact would have cost trust that is hard to win back.

Approach

  • Wrote a pre-send checklist and ran it every time: audience membership, channel opt-in, do-not-contact and opt-out overlap, open or won opportunities, replied suppression, and the send history for the same email.
  • Sent personalised tests to a real inbox and inspected the delivered body, the sender and signature mapping, and the desktop and mobile previews.
  • Confirmed the old flows were paused with no active version before activating the new ones.

What was built

  • A pre-send checklist with pass conditions, kept with the campaign.
  • Launch verification: every activation confirmed in Salesforce and every flow run accounted for.
  • A production delivery audit from raw engagement records: unique recipients, a duplicate check, bounces classified as hard rejection, mailbox full or organisation policy block, prior-hard-bounce suppressions honoured, and skipped profiles explained.

Outcome

The campaign went to exactly the approved audience once, every bounce has a cause, and the audit record stands for anyone who asks later.

Background

Most email mistakes are not deliverability failures, they are process failures: the wrong audience, a duplicate send, a message to someone who opted out, an old flow still running. The remedy is a checklist that runs before every send and an audit that runs after, so the team can prove what went where.

The problem in detail

The audience had been reviewed by hand and the older campaign flows were meant to be paused. In a platform where flows, segments and consent live in different places, the risk was a send that technically succeeded and practically failed: a duplicate to a contact already in a paused flow, or a message to someone whose consent had changed since the review.

How the work was done

The pre-send checklist checks membership in the approved list, opt-in for the channel, overlap with do-not-contact and opt-out fields, open or won opportunities, replied suppression, and the production send history for the same email. Personalised test sends went to a real inbox, where the delivered body, sender and signature mapping were inspected alongside desktop and mobile previews. The old flows were confirmed paused with no active version before the new ones were activated.

After launch, every activation was confirmed in Salesforce and every flow run accounted for. The delivery audit used raw engagement records rather than summary reports, because the summary tables had not yet populated.

What was delivered

A pre-send checklist with pass conditions, kept with the campaign. Launch verification with every activation and run accounted for. A production delivery audit: unique recipients, a duplicate check, bounces classified as hard rejection, mailbox full or organisation policy block, prior-hard-bounce suppressions honoured, and every skipped profile explained, including merged records with a second address that was correctly not sent.

Results

The campaign went to exactly the approved audience once, every bounce has a cause, and the audit record stands for anyone who asks later.

What this means for your team

  • Check consent and suppression at send time, not at review time. Consent changes between the two.
  • Send real tests to a real inbox and read the delivered message. Previews lie about signatures and merge fields.
  • Audit from raw records. Summary reports lag, and a launch audit cannot wait for them.

Questions about this project

What should a pre-send checklist cover?

Audience membership, channel opt-in, do-not-contact and opt-out overlap, open or won opportunities, replied suppression, send history for the same message, test sends to a real inbox, previews, and confirmation that older flows are paused.

How are bounces classified?

By cause: a hard rejection of the address, a full mailbox, or a block by the recipient organisation’s policy. Each needs a different response, and prior hard bounces stay suppressed.

Why audit from raw engagement records?

Because the reporting tables that summarise sends populate with a delay. An audit on launch day has to read the events themselves.
